What N5 Actually Covers

JLPT N5 is the entry level of the Japan Foundation's language proficiency test. It's commonly associated with roughly 800 core vocabulary words, around 100 basic kanji, and a set of foundational grammar patterns covering simple present/past tense, particles, and everyday sentence structures. It does not require fluency — it requires reliable command of the basics, which is exactly what this roadmap is built around.

ℹ️

A note on accuracy

Exact vocabulary/kanji counts are commonly cited approximations, not an official fixed list, and JLPT format details are set by the Japan Foundation. Always check the current official JLPT syllabus for the latest specifics.

The 6-Month Study Plan

MonthFocusGoal by End of Month
Month 1Hiragana, then katakanaRead both scripts fluently without sounding them out letter by letter
Month 2Core vocabulary + basic grammar (は, が, を, present tense)Build simple sentences and introduce yourself
Month 3Expand grammar (past tense, adjectives, て-form) + start kanjiHold a basic everyday conversation
Month 4Kanji (50+) + vocabulary expansion (500+ words)Read simple sentences with common kanji
Month 5Full grammar review + remaining kanji + listening practiceComplete full practice sections under timed conditions
Month 6Mock exams, weak-area review, exam strategyScore consistently above the passing threshold on mock tests
The students who pass comfortably aren't the ones who study the longest hours — they're the ones who show up every single day, even for just 30 minutes. Consistency beats intensity at the N5 stage. — Astha Pokhriyal, Founder & Lead Instructor

Recommended Daily Routine

You don't need hours a day — you need a routine you'll actually stick to. Here's what we recommend to our own students:

TimeActivity
10 minutesReview yesterday's vocabulary (spaced repetition)
15 minutesNew vocabulary or kanji (5–8 new items)
10 minutesGrammar pattern practice (writing example sentences)
10 minutesListening practice (audio, slow-paced content)

That's roughly 45 minutes a day — sustainable for six months, which matters far more than a two-hour session you abandon after a week.

Vocabulary Strategy

Learn vocabulary in themed batches (numbers, family, food, time, verbs) rather than random alphabetical lists — it's easier to retain and immediately usable in sentences. Use spaced repetition (reviewing a word at increasing intervals) rather than one-time memorisation. Our free 500 Essential JLPT N5 Vocabulary workbook is organised into 22 themed categories for exactly this purpose.

Kanji Strategy

Don't try to learn kanji in isolation from vocabulary — learn each kanji through words you already know. Stroke order matters more than it seems; it builds muscle memory that makes recognition faster later. Start with the highest-frequency ~50 kanji before worrying about the rest. Our free JLPT N5 Kanji Book covers every required kanji with readings, meanings and practice grids.

Grammar Strategy

Focus on patterns, not rules memorised in isolation — for every grammar point, write 3–5 of your own example sentences instead of just reading the explanation. The Minna no Nihongo textbook series is one of the most widely used structured resources for this stage, pairing each grammar point with practice dialogues; we've made a copy available in our free resources.

Practice Strategy

  • Weekly mock sections: from Month 4 onward, do at least one timed practice section every week.
  • Full mock exams: from Month 5, complete at least 2–3 full-length timed mock exams before the real test.
  • Track your weak areas: keep a simple log of question types you consistently get wrong and revisit them specifically, rather than re-studying everything equally.
  • Listening practice daily: even 10 minutes a day of slow-paced Japanese audio meaningfully improves listening-section performance over months.

Common Pitfalls

  • Skipping hiragana mastery and rushing into vocabulary while still sounding out letters — this slows down everything that follows.
  • Learning kanji in isolation from vocabulary, making them harder to recall in context.
  • Studying passively — rereading notes instead of actively writing, speaking and testing yourself.
  • No mock exams until the final week — leaving no time to fix weak areas before the real test.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to reach JLPT N5?

Most learners starting from zero reach a comfortable JLPT N5 level in 3 to 6 months with consistent daily study. Six months with a lighter daily load is a realistic, sustainable pace for most working adults and students.

How many words and kanji do I need for N5?

JLPT N5 is commonly associated with roughly 800 core vocabulary words and around 100 basic kanji, though exact figures can vary slightly by source. Our free 500-word vocabulary list and N5 kanji workbook cover the highest-frequency items first.

Should I learn hiragana and katakana at the same time?

We recommend mastering hiragana first since it appears far more frequently, then moving to katakana once hiragana feels comfortable — usually after 2 to 3 weeks. Trying to learn both simultaneously from day one tends to slow learners down rather than speed them up.

Is self-study enough to pass JLPT N5?

Yes, self-study with good structured resources is enough for many learners, especially with consistent daily practice and mock tests. Structured guidance helps most with pacing, pronunciation feedback and staying accountable — which is where a class or tutor adds the most value.
