Engineer Visa vs SSW vs TITP: Which Should You Choose?

Career growth, salary trajectory, path to permanent residency and language requirements — the direct comparison, no fluff.

Astha Pokhriyal, Founder & Lead Instructor ⏱ 5 min read 📅 Published 28 Jul 2026 🔄 Updated 28 Jul 2026

If you've read our guides on getting a job in Japan and the SSW visa, you already know there isn't one single "Japan work visa." Here's the direct comparison to help you pick a lane.

A note on accuracy

Visa rules and permanent residency pathways are set by Japan's Immigration Services Agency and can change. Treat this as a directional comparison and confirm current specifics on the official Immigration Services Agency website.

Who Should Choose What?

  • Choose Engineer/Specialist if you hold a relevant degree (or strong equivalent experience) and want the highest long-term salary ceiling and clearest path to PR — most common for IT and engineering roles.
  • Choose SSW if you don't have a degree but can commit to reaching JFT-Basic/N4 and passing an industry skills exam — the most realistic route into caregiving, food service, construction, manufacturing and similar sectors.
  • Consider TITP only if you're not yet ready for SSW's exam requirements and want a structured, training-first entry point — treat it as a possible stepping stone toward SSW, not a long-term plan.
Don't choose a visa route based on which one sounds easiest to get into. Choose it based on where you want to be in five years — the gap between these three only widens over time. — Astha Pokhriyal, Founder & Lead Instructor

Career Growth

The Engineer/Specialist visa offers the most room to grow — promotions, specialisation and job-switching within the same visa category are all straightforward. SSW allows renewal and, in fewer sectors, an upgrade to Type 2 with better long-term terms. TITP is the most limited: it's structured around a fixed training period rather than career progression, which is exactly why many workers use it as a stepping stone into SSW rather than an end goal.

Path to Permanent Residency

Engineer/Specialist visa holders and SSW Type 2 workers both have a realistic, if gradual, path toward permanent residency. SSW Type 1 does not lead directly to PR and has a capped total stay. TITP does not lead directly to PR either. If long-term settlement in Japan is your goal, that should weigh heavily in which route you start on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which visa is easiest to qualify for?

SSW is generally the most accessible for candidates without a university degree, since it's based on passing a language test and an industry skills exam rather than academic qualifications.

Which visa pays the most?

The Engineer/Specialist visa generally offers the highest salary ceiling, especially in IT, since it's tied to skilled, often specialised roles. SSW and TITP pay is closer to standard wages for their respective industries.

Can TITP lead to a better visa later?

Yes — many workers complete a TITP term and then transition into SSW using the practical experience and, in some cases, exemptions from parts of the SSW skills exam gained during training.

Which visa has the clearest path to permanent residency?

The Engineer/Specialist visa and SSW Type 2 both offer paths toward permanent residency over time, generally with fewer restrictions than SSW Type 1. TITP does not lead directly to PR. Rules in this area change periodically, so confirm current details on Japan's official Immigration Services Agency website.
