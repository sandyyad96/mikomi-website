A note on accuracy

Salaries vary by employer, prefecture, experience and the exact job role, and Japan's minimum wage differs by prefecture and is revised periodically. The figures below are commonly cited general ranges for planning purposes, not a guarantee for any specific offer.

Don't compare a headline salary number across industries without also comparing the work itself — physical demands, hours, and accommodation arrangements differ just as much as the pay does. — Astha Pokhriyal, Founder & Lead Instructor

What Affects Your Salary

  • Prefecture: Tokyo and other major cities generally pay more than rural areas, partly to offset higher living costs.
  • Experience & tenure: Pay typically increases with time in role, especially in Engineer/IT and SSW Type 2 positions.
  • Company size: Larger employers often (not always) pay more and offer more structured benefits than small businesses.
  • Overtime: Many roles include overtime pay, which can meaningfully change actual monthly take-home compared to base salary alone.
  • Accommodation arrangements: Some employers provide subsidised or free housing, which changes your effective savings even if the salary number looks similar to another offer.

Taxes & Deductions

Japan deducts several items from gross salary before you see your take-home pay:

  • Income tax — progressive, based on your income level
  • Resident tax — a local tax, usually starting from your second year of residence
  • Health insurance — mandatory enrolment for most work visa categories
  • Pension contributions — mandatory national pension enrolment

Combined, these commonly reduce gross pay by roughly 15–25%, though the exact figure depends on income level, dependents and municipality. Treat this as a planning estimate — your actual payslip is the only accurate source.

Savings Potential

How much you can actually save depends far more on your city and lifestyle than on your salary alone. A ¥200,000 salary in a smaller city with employer-provided housing can leave more disposable income than a ¥250,000 salary in central Tokyo with market-rate rent. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average salary in Japan for Indians?

It varies enormously by industry and visa route — SSW roles typically pay ¥160,000 to ¥250,000 gross per month depending on the sector, while Engineer/IT visa roles commonly range from ¥250,000 to over ¥450,000 depending on experience and company. There is no single "average" that applies to everyone.

How much can I realistically save each month in Japan?

After typical deductions and living costs, many SSW workers save a meaningful portion of their income, especially outside major cities where rent is lower. Savings potential depends heavily on your city, lifestyle and whether accommodation is employer-provided — treat any specific savings figure as a rough estimate, not a guarantee.

How much tax will be deducted from my salary in Japan?

Japan deducts income tax, resident tax and social insurance (health insurance and pension) from salary, and the combined effect commonly reduces gross pay by roughly 15-25%, though this varies by income level, dependents and municipality. Always check your actual payslip breakdown rather than relying on a general estimate.
