A note on accuracy

Prices vary by neighbourhood, building age, season and provider, and can change over time. The figures below are commonly cited general ranges for planning purposes, not fixed prices.

City Comparison at a Glance

City1R Apartment Rent (Monthly)Overall Cost Level
Tokyo¥70,000 – ¥120,000+Highest
Osaka¥50,000 – ¥85,000Medium-high
Nagoya¥45,000 – ¥75,000Medium
Fukuoka¥40,000 – ¥65,000Lowest of the four

Rent is the single biggest driver of cost differences between these cities — everything else (food, transport, utilities) varies far less by comparison.

Choosing which city to work in matters almost as much as choosing which job — the same salary can feel completely different depending on where your rent goes. — Astha Pokhriyal, Founder & Lead Instructor

A Sample Monthly Budget

Here's an illustrative budget for a single SSW worker earning around ¥200,000 gross, living in a mid-sized city like Nagoya with market-rate (not employer-provided) housing:

ItemApproximate Cost
Rent (1R apartment)¥55,000
Food¥30,000
Transport¥7,000
Utilities + internet + mobile¥15,000
Estimated tax & social insurance~¥40,000
Remaining (potential savings)~¥53,000

This is a simplified illustration, not a calculation for any specific individual — actual numbers depend heavily on your city, housing arrangement and lifestyle. We walk through the salary side of this in our industry salary guide.

How Much Can You Realistically Save?

With employer-provided or shared housing — common in many SSW placements — the rent line in the budget above can drop dramatically, often turning into the single biggest factor in how much you save each month. Workers in Fukuoka or Nagoya with subsidised housing frequently save a noticeably larger share of their income than someone on a similar salary paying full market rent in central Tokyo.

Tips to Reduce Costs

  • Ask about employer-provided housing before comparing job offers on salary alone.
  • Cook at home — eating out regularly is one of the fastest ways monthly food costs climb.
  • Use a commuter pass if your employer doesn't already cover it — it's usually cheaper than per-ride fares.
  • Consider a smaller city if your job type allows it — Fukuoka and Nagoya offer meaningfully lower rent than Tokyo or Osaka for a similar lifestyle.
  • Share accommodation where possible, especially in your first months, to reduce the single largest expense.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the cheapest major city to live in Japan?

Among major hubs, Fukuoka is generally the most affordable, followed by Nagoya, with Osaka in the middle and Tokyo consistently the most expensive, mainly due to rent. Costs vary by specific neighbourhood, so treat these as general city-level patterns, not fixed rules.

How much should I budget per month in Japan?

For a single person in a smaller city with employer-provided or shared housing, a monthly budget of roughly ¥120,000–¥160,000 covering rent, food, transport and utilities is a common general estimate. In Tokyo, the same lifestyle often costs meaningfully more due to rent alone.

Does employer-provided housing make a big difference?

Yes, significantly. Since rent is usually the single largest expense, subsidised or free employer-provided housing can be the biggest factor in how much you're able to save each month, sometimes more impactful than the base salary difference between job offers.
